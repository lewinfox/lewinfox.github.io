# Overview

This website is built with [`zola`](https://www.getzola.org/documentation) and
is a business site promoting my software and data consultancy, based in
Queenstown, New Zealand. It's deployed to GitHub Pages by
`.github/workflows/deploy.yml` on every push to `main`.

A development server is normally running (`make serve` serves on
`localhost:8080`; `zola serve` in the devcontainer uses `localhost:1111`); use
`curl` if you need to inspect a page or test anything.

Site CSS is defined in `sass/style.scss`. Any styling changes should be applied
to this file rather than in any CSS or HTML files.

# Site contents

- Homepage (`content/_index.md`)
- Services (`content/services/_index.md`)
- Portfolio (`content/portfolio/_index.md`)
- About (`content/about/_index.md`)
- Blog (`content/blog/`)

There is no contact page or form: calls to action are `mailto:` links to
lewin.a.f@gmail.com.

## Layouts

- `templates/landing.html`: full-screen photo with the text in the lower half.
  Used by the homepage, Services and About. Each page picks a photo with
  `[extra] photo = "hero" | "lake" | "paragliders"` in its front matter; photo files
  are in `static/img/` and their Unsplash credits are under `[extra.photos]` in
  `config.toml`.
- `templates/section.html` and `templates/page.html`: plain dark layout with a
  photo band across the top, for the blog and Portfolio. The photo is
  `reading_photo` in `config.toml` (autumn trees) unless a section sets its own
  with `[extra] photo = "..."`, as Portfolio does ("lakeside"). Each page should
  have a different photo; a new photo needs an entry under `[extra.photos]` and
  a `.photo-<name>` class in `sass/style.scss`.
- `templates/base.html`: shared `<head>`, header and footer.

The fox logo in the header is `templates/partials/logo.html`, generated by
`tools/fox-logo.py`.

## Portfolio

One page listing apps I've built. Each app is a `## Heading`, one paragraph
saying what the app does in plain terms, and a strip of screenshots. To add an
app:

1. Put its screenshots (PNG) in `static/img/portfolio/<app>/`.
2. Add a heading, a paragraph and a `gallery` to `content/portfolio/_index.md`,
   copying an existing entry. Each gallery line is
   `"<file name without .png> | <description of the screenshot>"`.
3. Link to it from the project list on the About page with
   `[Name](@/portfolio/_index.md#name)`.

The `gallery` component is defined in `templates/components/gallery.html`
(one file per component in that folder). Zola
generates the small WebP versions into `static/processed_images/` at build
time; that folder is git-ignored.

## Headers and footers

All pages contain a header and footer. The header contains a link to my GitHub,

## Who the site is for

Local people and businesses with a technical problem to solve, who have
searched for something like "software development Queenstown", "app developer
Queenstown" or "web development Queenstown". They usually aren't technical, so
copy should describe problems and outcomes in their terms, not technology.

Page titles, headings and meta descriptions should use the phrases they search
for ("software / app / web development", "Queenstown") where it reads
naturally. Don't keyword-stuff.

## Homepage and Services

The proposition, in my words: **I build software, any kind of software, to
solve a business problem, and I'm easy to communicate with.** Every page should
serve that and nothing else.

- The problem leads, not the technology. Apps, websites and web applications
  are examples of what the answer might be, not a menu of products.
- "Easy to communicate with" comes across from how the site reads (short,
  plain, no jargon) and from specifics like being local and meeting in person.
  Never claim it outright ("I explain things in plain English") or spell out
  what the site already implies ("you deal with me directly").
- I can also host and maintain what I build. Every project is quoted
  individually; don't publish prices or rates.
- My background is in data engineering, but visitors are unlikely to be looking
  for that (it's mostly bought by larger firms), so data work is mentioned only
  as part of the wider offer, in small-business terms: spreadsheets, reporting.
  Don't headline it or list data tooling (dbt, Snowflake, etc.) on the main
  pages; the blog can go deeper.

## Tone of voice

Applies to all site copy and blog posts. Based on the existing pages and the
foodwebr release post (`content/blog/2025-10-01-foodwebr-1.0.0.md`).

**Who's talking.** Me, in the first person ("I build...", "I'm happy to meet in
person"). I'm a sole trader, so never "we", "our team" or a company voice.
Address the reader as "you".

**Plain and concrete.** Say what the thing is and what it does in ordinary
words. Prefer a specific example to an abstract claim: "replacing a paper form
or a spreadsheet with something your team can fill in on their phones" beats
"streamlining your business processes".

**Friendly, not salesy.** Warm and direct, like explaining something to a
client over coffee. Confident about what I can do without hype. One clear call
to action, stated simply ("Get in touch"); low-key asks
elsewhere ("If you find foodwebr useful, consider starring the repository").

**Short.** Minimal verbiage. Short sentences and short paragraphs. Landing
pages are a heading and two or three short paragraphs, around 50 words
(About can run longer: it's where my background and who I am go). Cut
anything that doesn't tell the reader something new. If a sentence could appear
on any developer's website, cut it.

**Show, don't tell (technical posts).** Lead with what the thing is and why
you'd care, then get to something the reader can run. Code examples should be
complete, copy-pasteable, and show their output where it helps. A typical
shape: what it is → how to install or set it up → a minimal example → the
more useful options → where to learn more.

**Avoid:**

- Stock openers and filler: "I'm excited to announce", "In today's
  fast-paced world", "makes it easy/simple", "at a glance", "seamless",
  "robust", "leverage", "unlock", "empower".
- Buzzwords and jargon the reader hasn't asked for. If a technical term is
  needed, use it plainly; don't stack them.
- Bolded-label bullet lists ("**Easy visualization**: ...") as a substitute for
  explaining. Use a list only for genuinely list-shaped things.
- Emoji and overclaiming ("best-in-class", "cutting-edge").
- Exclamation marks are fine if used sparingly and they sound human ("Hi! I'm
  Lewin."). No more than one on a page, and never to add excitement to a sales
  line.
- AI-sounding patterns: "not X, but Y" contrasts ("the right tool, not the
  newest one"), lists of three for rhythm, statements of values or philosophy
  ("I like building practical things..."), whimsy ("wandered off the track"),
  corporate phrases ("discuss your needs", "look forward to"), and em dashes.
- Describing myself with adjectives (friendly, pragmatic, reliable). Say what I
  do instead.

**Spelling.** New Zealand English ("organisation", "visualise", "colour").
Keep the original spelling for code, package names and quoted output.

## SEO and metadata

- Every page should have a front matter `description` (one or two plain
  sentences: what's on the page, plus "Queenstown, New Zealand" where it fits).
  It's used for the meta description and link previews.
- On landing pages the front matter `title` is only used in `<title>` (the
  visible heading is the `# ...` in the Markdown), so make it descriptive, e.g.
  Services is "Apps, websites and data engineering".
- `templates/base.html` builds the `<title>`, meta description, canonical URL
  and Open Graph tags. `templates/index.html` has the business/person structured
  data (JSON-LD), and `templates/page.html` has `BlogPosting` structured data.
  Update the JSON-LD in `index.html` if the services or location change.
- `static/llms.txt` is a hand-written plain-text summary of me and the site for
  LLMs. Keep it in sync when services, pricing or pages change.
- The link preview image is `static/img/social.jpg`, generated by
  `tools/social-image.py`.
- Draft content (`draft = true`) is left out of the build and sitemap. The
  case studies in `content/case-studies/` are drafts until they're ready.

## Design principles

The website should be as light as possible, content-first. Minimal JS, maximum
simplicity, clear navigation, simple tech stack. I am not a web developer and
want something simple and maintainable.

## Notes for Claude

The `Makefile` contains targets for `build` and `serve`. Don't run these, but
be aware that we are using a Docker container to make the developer experience
as clean as possible. Assume there is always a live server running during
development; you do NOT need to build or serve the site at any point.
